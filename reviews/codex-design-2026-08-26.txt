## 1. Edit model

Replace the footer link with a persistent **Edit campaign** button:

- Desktop: gold-filled, 48px-high button in the header’s upper-right corner; remains visible in a sticky 64px navigation bar.
- Phone: fixed bottom-right button, **Edit campaign**, 52px high—not an icon-only FAB.
- Once activated: label changes to **Editing**, every editable control becomes visible, and a sticky bottom bar shows **Changes · Publish · Exit**.

Editable campaign information:

- Add a festival recommendation.
- Promote a recommendation to the active campaign.
- Move a festival between Active, Recommended, and Ruled out; restore it later.
- Change priority tier: Dream, Strong, Worthy.
- Record Submitted, Selected, Not selected, Withdrawn, Screened, Nominated, or Won.
- Record event date, actual fee/currency, confirmation number/link, award name, and public-safe notes.
- Set the next action and personal reminder date.
- Reserve or spend premiere rights and associate them with a festival.
- Edit the director-owned “Why KOM” note.

Locked research:

- Festival name and edition.
- Location/category.
- Festival dates and submission windows.
- Published fee schedule.
- Eligibility and qualifying path.
- Official site and FilmFreeway URLs.
- Research assessment, source, estimated/verified flag, and last-checked date.

Locked fields appear with a small lock and **Verified Aug 25**. They are never inputs in ordinary edit mode. A secondary **Suggest research correction** flow requires a replacement source URL and records a pending correction—it does not silently alter published facts.

New festivals enter as purple **Needs research** recommendations with only three editable fields: name, webpage link, and “recommended by/why.” They cannot become active targets until promoted.

## 2. The row and the card

### Desktop row

Replace the current nine-column table with an 88px status-first summary row:

1. **6px status rail**
2. **Status, 144px:** large pill plus event date
3. **Festival, flexible:** 18px name; location/category beneath; tier shown only as an 8px dot and label
4. **Next deadline, 150px:** 22px countdown over the exact date
5. **Links, 230px:** two 44px-high buttons—**Official website ↗** and **FilmFreeway ↗**
6. **Chevron, 44px target:** expands details

The expanded row contains the locked research record, Why KOM, submission history, notes, and premiere implications. External-link buttons are never hidden inside that expansion.

In edit mode:

- The status pill becomes a 44px-high selector.
- **Next action** and **Reminder** appear inline.
- A 44px overflow button exposes priority, notes, move/rule-out, and premiere controls.
- Locked research remains read-only.

### Mobile card

Use a full-width card with a minimum 156px resting height:

- 32px status banner across the top.
- Festival name at 20px, wrapping to two lines.
- Deadline as the hero: **“6 days left”** at 28px, with **Aug 31 · $95** below.
- Tier, location, and qualifying path as quiet metadata.
- Two side-by-side 48px buttons: **Festival website** and **FilmFreeway**.
- A clearly labeled 44px **View details** row at the bottom.

Tapping the card body opens a bottom-sheet detail view; tapping a link opens only that link. In edit mode, the banner becomes **Change status**, and **Next action** appears above the links.

## 3. Visual information delivery

Status owns the strongest color and weight:

- Muted gray: not submitted.
- Gold: submitted / awaiting decision.
- Green: selected / screened.
- Red: not selected.
- Purple: recommended / needs research.
- Withdrawn: gray with strike treatment.
- Winner: green status plus a gold star—not another competing status color.

Tier hues become supporting information: small dot, slim timeline bar, or quiet label. They should not tint entire rows.

Across the page:

- Replace eight equally loud KPI tiles with a pipeline: **Targets → Submitted → Pending → Selected → Wins**, using the same status colors and counts.
- Dream List rows and mobile cards always carry the status rail/banner.
- Timeline submission windows recede to 35% tier color. Overlay event markers: gold submission dot, green selection/screening diamond, red rejection mark.
- Selected and pending festivals retain full opacity; closed, ruled-out, and rejected records recede.
- Deadline urgency earns large type, not a second red status system. Use gold under seven days; red only when overdue and still actionable.
- Research prose, sources, and timestamps use muted text. Official links remain high-contrast gold-outlined controls.
- Every color is paired with a label or icon.

## 4. Interaction flows

### Mark submitted — 4 phone taps

1. **Edit campaign**
2. Festival’s **Change status**
3. **Submitted** — date defaults to today
4. **Publish**

A toast offers **Add fee/details** and **Undo** without blocking the fast path.

### Record a decision — 4 phone taps

1. **Edit campaign**
2. Festival’s status control
3. **Official selection** or **Not selected**
4. **Publish**

The decision date defaults to today. Selection reveals optional award, screening, and premiere-reservation fields in the same sheet; no second modal cascade.

### Add a recommended festival — 4 action taps plus typing

1. **Edit campaign**
2. Sticky **+ Add festival**
3. Enter name, webpage link, and recommendation note
4. **Add to recommendations**, then **Publish** from the persistent bar

It appears immediately in the purple Needs Research section, not among verified targets.

## 5. What not to do

- No footer-only edit entry or unexplained pencil icons.
- No editable mega-form containing sourced research.
- No tiny “site” text links or links hidden under “details.”
- No horizontal nine-column table on phones.
- No full-card link that conflicts with expanding or editing.
- No tier-colored rows masquerading as campaign status.
- No color-only status communication.
- No raw labels such as `disposition`, `addLead`, or `stage`.
- No automatic promotion of a recommendation into the active campaign.
- No mandatory Review screen before every publish; review remains optional.
- No chain of modals after recording a selection.
