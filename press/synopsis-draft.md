# Synopsis — first drafts

*Sep 8, 2026. The EPK's page 2 needs a logline **and** a ~100-word synopsis, and the synopsis has never been written. These are drafts from the facts already on record. Nothing invented — where a fact is needed and missing, it is bracketed.*

**What a synopsis does that a logline does not:** it may carry the arc, name the turn, and end somewhere the logline only points at. It is read by a programmer who has already decided the logline was interesting.

---

## A — the plain one · 98 words

> South Carolina, 1834. Mace is the best fighter on the plantation, which is why he is still alive. His owner stages bare-knuckle bouts against other enslaved men and wagers on the outcome; Mace wins, and winning is the arrangement that keeps him out of the field and above the ground.
>
> After a fight that ends in a man's death, he follows the body and finds a burial being held in secret, in the Yoruba rites of the ancestors he and the dead man share. Among the mourners is an elder who tells him what he would have been across the water.
>
> He has to decide what he is now.

## B — the one that leads with the world · 101 words

> On the plantations of 1830s South Carolina, enslaved men are matched against each other in fights to the death, staged and wagered on by the men who own them. Mace has never lost.
>
> When a bout ends in a killing, he stumbles on the dead man's burial, held in secret in the faith of their shared ancestors — the one ceremony on that land his owners have no claim on. An elder there tells him who he would have been in the motherland.
>
> What the fighting has kept alive in him, and what it has taken, are no longer separable.

## C — the one that follows Mace · 96 words

> Mace fights because the alternative is the field, and then the ground. On a South Carolina plantation in 1834 his owner stages bouts against other enslaved men and takes the wagers; Mace has never lost, and every win buys him another season.
>
> Then a fight ends in a death, and he follows the body to a burial held in secret in the Yoruba faith he was born to. The rites are for a man he killed. An elder tells him what he would have been across the water, and he sees, for the first time, what he has become.
