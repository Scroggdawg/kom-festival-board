## Recommended (EPK, 157 words)

Cinematographer Luke Scroggins shot KILLER OF MEN, his thesis film at the AFI Conservatory, for director Jordan Betine. The 1830s South Carolina drama was shot on the ARRI Alexa 35, on a farm, in a barn and on a soundstage. For director John Lavelle he shot the short KEEPING WARM, one continuous scene inside a minivan. His AFI cycle films are PLACENTA for director Japhet Velazquez, PROSPECT, a motorcycle-club crime drama, for Piper De Palma, and DEEP TISSUE, an action thriller, for Matthew Genecov. With director Ze'ev Waismann he shot LE'DIRECTOR, his AFI application film, and commercials for Ford Bronco, the U.S. Air Force and Banner Health. Before AFI he was camera utility and data manager on the feature FRESH KILLS under cinematographer Ben Hardwicke. He holds a BFA in Acting from Otterbein University and appeared on SWITCHED AT BIRTH, GENERAL HOSPITAL and DAYS OF OUR LIVES. He is from San Antonio and based in Los Angeles.

## Method

Sixty-four agents in one workflow. Seven research sweeps read 217 real bios and guidance pages: cinematographers' own sites, agency rosters, emerging-DP features, festival catalogues and press kits, career-changer bios, hiring guidance, director bios and a cliché census. One agent turned that into a brief with a seven-criterion rubric and a 40-phrase banned list. Eight drafters wrote from eight angles. Each draft was split into claims and checked against a fact sheet built from Luke's own files, then scored by three judges. A synthesizer built the package from the top draft. Three refuters checked it four times; the last objections were to note text, not to the bios.

The seven findings the bios are built on:

1. Every credit is a unit: title, director, year. A format list ("narrative, commercial, music video") was the most common substitute in 19 of 45 DP bios read, and in none of the director bios.
2. Lead with the current film in a past-tense verb, not with birthplace or school.
3. Counts beat adjectives.
4. Name repeat collaborators.
5. One process fact per headline credit.
6. Commercials in their own sentence with brands and the director named.
7. Acting as one clause after the credits, framed as fact. Every acting-to-DP bio read reduces it to a clause; none claims transferred skill.

Most festival surfaces print no DP bio at all, only a credit line. The EPK bio is a post-selection press asset that a catalogue editor will trim, so nothing in it should be discountable.
