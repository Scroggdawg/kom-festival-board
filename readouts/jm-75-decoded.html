J M's playbook, decoded

Student category, always

Nearly every pick enters the student or college lane: cheaper fees, and the thesis competes against student work instead of professional shorts. Better odds, same laurel.

Volume of cheap lottery tickets

Many small qualifying festivals at $5–$40 instead of a few prestige swings. deadCenter, SLIFF, In the Palace, Drama — each one a real Oscar path for the price of lunch.

The Black circuit is a ladder

Nineteen Black or African-diaspora festivals, from Newark (running since 1974) to Greenwood (Black Wall Street). Community wins compound into a following — not a tag, a strategy.

Regional premiere rules are currency

The picks skew toward festivals whose premiere bar is a city or a state — Birmingham, St. Louis, Cleveland, the Bay Area. Prior screenings elsewhere cost nothing there.

Perks are budget

Notes track fee waivers, student discounts, hotels, travel stipends, cash prizes. Anonimul and ISC Osaka fly you in; Virginia costs $2.50. The campaign pays for itself where it can.

What the sheet lacks

No sequencing layer. Four picks fight over one LA premiere, roughly a dozen resolve to editions that miss the 100th-Oscars window, and five qualifying claims don't check out. Volume needs chess on top.

The LA collision. Slamdance (Feb), PAFF (Feb), LA Shorts (Jul), and HollyShorts (Aug) all sit on J M's list — but HollyShorts requires an LA premiere and LA Shorts requires no prior LA County screening. The film gets exactly one LA debut. Playing PAFF or Slamdance in February forfeits the August lane, and the board's premiere ledger has its own plan for LA. Sequence before submitting to any of the four.
