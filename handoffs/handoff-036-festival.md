# handoff-036-festival

## Where we left off

**This turn (Sep 7, 2026, Opus 5 — Luke switched the model; ultracode on):** Luke asked for ~20 **found** loglines from historical dramas in Killer of Men's territory, plus a second group of loglines famous for being great. Emphasis his: *"You're not writing these, you're finding them."*

Launched a six-lane research workflow (`logline-corpus`, run `wf_831e704b-63f`), still running at the time of writing:

| Lane | Scope |
|---|---|
| `antebellum` | American slavery / antebellum drama — 12 Years a Slave, Beloved, Amistad, Harriet, Sankofa, Nightjohn, The Retrieval, Underground, Belle… |
| `nineteenth` | 19th-century and Civil War drama — Cold Mountain, The Revenant, Hostiles, The Keeping Room, The Nightingale, First Cow… |
| `fight-diaspora` | Films built on organised violence (Raging Bull, Warrior, Fat City, A Prayer Before Dawn) + Black diaspora/African historical (The Woman King, Camp de Thiaroye, Atlantics) |
| `shorts` | **The most relevant lane** — festival programme loglines for award-winning narrative shorts, written by filmmakers for submission, which is exactly Luke's task |
| `canon` | Loglines famous in screenwriting teaching, plus Black List / spec-sale wordings |
| `craft` | The rules from named authorities, with disagreements between them recorded |

**The methodological point, which is the whole reason for the fan-out:** most loglines circulating online are written by bloggers to illustrate a point, not published by the production. Every entry carries a source tier (`official` / `database` / `screenwriting_source` / `reconstruction`), a URL, and a `kind` (logline vs tagline vs first line of a synopsis). Each lane has an adversarial verifier that fetches the URL, checks the text is verbatim, downgrades dishonest tiers, and **drops** anything it cannot substantiate. A completeness critic then reports duplicates, still-doubtful entries, missing titles, the six most instructive for KOM, and a counted pattern analysis (how many open on setting, how many name the protagonist, typical word count, how many end on theme vs plot).
