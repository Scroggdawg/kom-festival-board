When he stumbles upon the burial rites of the opponent he just killed, he finds the ceremony being held in the ancient faith of their shared ancestors, who he must now face to decide whether or not he will kill again.

Seven changes from Gen 20. Two were notes of mine taken: the broken *"But every victory,"* is rebuilt as *"But every victory means taking"*, and *"face and decide"* is back to *"face to decide"*. The capital-F flag is answered — *faith* is lowercase now. *"Stumbling"* reverted to *"When he stumbles"*, restoring the active verb.

### The three open notes

1. **"whether or not" — cut it.** "Whether" already contains "or not"; the phrase is only legitimate when it means *regardless of whether*, which is not the meaning here. Two words for nothing in the position that has to land hardest. **69 words without it.**
2. **"and survival" is the only new idea, and it is worth it.** It supplies the motive sentence 2 needs — he wins because losing kills him — which turns "he must take the life of one of his own" from an atrocity into a trap. The cost: pure objectification was the more damning image. His trade to make. The comma before *and* is emphasis, not standard punctuation, and will read to some as a dictation artifact.
3. **"opponent" is a small step back from "man".** *"The man he just killed"* pays off *"one of his own"* — same kind of man as the "enslaved man" in sentence 1. "Opponent" re-files him as a role in a fight, which is the framing the film argues against. It buys only the avoidance of a second "man."
