### The disagreement, answered — and the taxonomy agreed independently

Luke is right it is not man-vs-man. He is wrong that "man versus himself" names the machine: **the loglines that most explicitly announce internal conflict are in the failure bucket** (Ford v Ferrari "their own personal demons"; A Star Is Born "his own internal demons"; Waltz with Bashir "one man's journey to uncover his past"). The real thing is **causal irony** — one cause, two opposite valences. Proof from the grammar: of the 15 entries using "but" as a hinge, **all 15 put it between the two effects; not one entry in 119 puts an adversative between a man and his own interior.**

### Two findings that change his draft

1. **My arithmetic was wrong and I corrected it.** I said the locked pieces were "about 30 words." Counted exactly, they are **48** (7 + 18 + 8 + 15). With minimum connective tissue, all four locks floor the logline at **60–64 words. Under 50 is arithmetically impossible with all four.**
2. **The choice ending competes with the machine.** `the_choice` is the smallest working class (11/119) and structurally cancels the irony: if the violence has already been costing him his humanity with every victory, the question of who he is has been answered before the choice is offered. **Zero of the 19 trait_two_consequences entries carry a choice clause; zero of the purest seven do.** Releasing the ending buys 15 words. A three-lock arrangement of his own words comes to **49**.

### Other findings

- **Virtue, not vice.** The machine works when the trait is a skill or virtue the reader would want (accuracy, bravado, honesty, perfectionism). It degrades on vices — appetite produced 6 of the 19 machine entries but **zero of the purest seven**, because a vice needs no crediting. KOM's trait (violence as survival skill) is on the virtue side.
- **His phrasing already solved a problem he had not named.** A coerced trait cannot be credited with a victory proudly, which is why Kapò and The Grey Zone reach for price_named. "The violence that **has kept him alive**" credits survival, not glory — the coerced man's version of the machine, and correct for this film.
- **"a little more of his humanity" is the one hedge.** The corpus never hedges costs ("even his humanity", "at any price", "in exchange for a few more months of life"); 20 entries name the cost as a person or object.
- **Device to use:** the relative clause (4 of the purest 7; the only device costing nothing per extra idea) plus an em dash. Not "but" (15/99, costs a clause). **Not a semicolon — 0 of 119.**
- **Worlds to search next:** medicine/surgery; coerced collaboration under slavery and occupation (KOM's actual genre, thin here at 5 entries but 2 of them among the strongest in 119); law; athletics and performance outside combat.

Idea box Generation 8 logged earlier this turn, including "entreats his most brutal nature" (flagged: *entreat* means to beg — check he did not mean *embraces*) and the unresolved "from him" vs "from his people" (a third reading offered: leave it as "the life that was taken", which lets it mean both the life slavery took from Mace and the life Mace just took).
