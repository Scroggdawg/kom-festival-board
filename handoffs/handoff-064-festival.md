## Where we left off

**This turn (Sep 8, 2026, Opus 5):** Luke set the decision — **"whether he will kill again"** — and offered two versions of the final sentence, asking which.

**The decision is the right one and is the best written in eighteen generations:** concrete, showable inside thirteen minutes, and it promises no chosen death the film may not contain (which was the standing flag on "how his life will end").

### Recommended **B** over **A**, three reasons

A reads *"…the ancient faith of their shared ancestors, **he is faced with the spirits of his past**…"*

1. **It names them twice and the second naming is weaker** — a specific noun (ancestors) traded for a vaguer one (spirits of his past) after the reader already knows who they are.
2. **"His past" files them as history.** In Yoruba practice the ancestors are present, not past, and that presence is why the scene works. Same distancing risk "ancient" carried, this time with no factual defence.
3. Six words longer for less.

B's *"who he must now face"* points back at *ancestors* with one pronoun — no re-naming, no drift, and they stay the grammatical object of "face."

### Four versions logged (idea box Generation 18)

A 65 · B 60 · C 59 (hybrid, keeps his "When" beat with "he must face them") · **D 56 (tightest)**.

**Recommendation: D**, or B (60) if his exact wording is preferred. D takes his "When" opening, drops "and finds it" to the participial "held in" (saves three words), and keeps "face them" pointing at the ancestors. Every word is his except the participle.

> In 1834 South Carolina, an enslaved man is forced to fight for spectacle. But with every victory, he must take the life of one of his own. When he stumbles upon his fallen opponent's burial ceremony, held in the ancient faith of their shared ancestors, he must face them to decide whether he will kill again.
