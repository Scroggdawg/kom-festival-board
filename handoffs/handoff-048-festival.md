## Where we left off

**This turn (Sep 7, 2026, night, Opus 5, ultracode):** Luke rejected the whole "a right he has lost" direction (*"no right to mourn"*, *"the right to be buried"*, *"grieve a man he killed"*, *"a faith he has no right to claim"* — all *"not that enticing"*), noted I had worked too fast, and asked for deep work with several review passes and an explicit warning against churn: *"don't change it just for the sake of changing it — in the review you might nail it the first time."*

Ran an 11-agent panel: **six writers on separate angles** (world-as-hook · violence/ancestral collision · his own words rearranged · maximum compression · camera-only-no-abstractions · the Raging Bull machine) → **36 loglines** → **three reviewers** each scoring all 36 (enticement alone · fidelity to his rulings · purely adversarial) → **a reviser** under no-churn orders → **a final adversary** that attacked the six finals and **cut three**.

### The three survivors, fixes applied — `press/logline-final-three.md`

- **A, 52 words, recommended.** "…survives by killing his own. **Every victory buys him another night and costs a man his life.** He walks in on that man's burial…" The crux machine with the abstraction removed; the cost is a man's life and *that man* is whose funeral he enters two words later. Safest; carries no ancestral world.
- **B, 44 words, the risk.** "…**opens onto a world the men who own him cannot buy.**" The only version where the ancestral world is *jurisdiction* — one square foot the owners have no claim on — rather than decoration. "Will to live" is conduct, so it can damn him.
- **C, 58 words.** "**wins because losing is dying**… and **an elder tells him what he would have been in the motherland.**" The only one that says what losing costs and the only one ending on a scene.

**Adversary's best phrase of the night:** *"a world the men who own him cannot enter"* (fixed to *"cannot buy"* — turns sanctuary into a claim they cannot win and puts the wagering back).

### The three cut, and why they matter as lessons

1. "hardens him into a stranger at the graves of his own people" — **the estrangement note he already refused, in a better coat**, and interior, which is the framing he killed.
2. "physical dominance… his own people gather in secret to bury another man" — **Mace is not in his own logline**; the one indelible image is him standing at that funeral. Also "physical dominance" is a skill (costs nothing) and is the exact frame Jordan's statement exists to dismantle.
3. "his hands take longer to open" — beautiful, but the cost is a physical symptom, the cheapest currency available.

### The finding that outranks further rewriting

The set cannot improve by word choice alone. **Three facts are needed and only Luke and Jordan have them:**
1. **Who was the man he killed?** All six call him a category. *Son of Saul* works because the corpse is "a boy he takes for his son." A name, a shared field, or that Mace knows the song would beat every remaining word change.
2. **One concrete noun from inside the surreal sequence** — no line says what he sees on the other side.
3. **Does he fight again after the burial?** If so, that is the forward beat the whole set lacks, and it is one clause.
