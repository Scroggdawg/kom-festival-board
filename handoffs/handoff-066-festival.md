### The four edits

1. **"forced to fight to the death for spectacle"** — a gain. "Fight for spectacle" left open *how*; three words answer it and set stakes before sentence 2 confirms them. Cost: sentence 2 now partly restates sentence 1 and leans entirely on "one of his own." It still earns its place.
2. **"But every victory"** (dropping *with*) — **broken, do not ship.** "Every victory" has no grammatical job: not a prepositional phrase, and it cannot be the subject because the subject is "he." Reads as a dictation drop, not a choice.
3. **"Stumbling upon the burial rites of the man he just killed, he finds the ceremony"** — split. "Rites" beats "ceremony" (specific, religious, pre-loads "the ancient Faith"). "The man he just killed" beats "his fallen opponent" (that is a euphemism; this film has no use for one). But the sentence trades "ceremony" out and back in one clause later, and "Stumbling" demotes the stronger verb to a participle, leaving "finds" to carry it.
4. **"face and decide"** — a loss. "Face **to** decide" makes the confrontation the means of deciding; "and" makes them two items on a list. The causal reading is truer to the film.

**The merge** keeps 1 and 3, restores *with* and *to decide*:

> In 1834 South Carolina, an enslaved man is forced to fight to the death for spectacle. But with every victory, he must take the life of one of his own. Stumbling upon the burial rites of the man he just killed, he finds the ceremony being held in the ancient Faith of their shared ancestors, who he must now face to decide whether he will kill again.
