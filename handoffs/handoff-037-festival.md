### The finding that should change the draft

The critic counted 30 KOM-adjacent loglines rather than asserting patterns:

- **Ends on theme: only 33%** — and 3 of the 4 antebellum entries that do land on the literal words "freedom", "roots" or "past". **Luke's v4 logline ends on "ancestral roots, guilt, faith and the violence"** — the most worn close in his exact lane.
- **States the irony or cost: 85% of shorts.** The single strongest correlate of a logline that works, and the one element a short cannot omit (no runtime to promise it later).
- **Length: mean 36.8, median 36.** Luke's v4 is 50 — legal (Sundance allows 75) but long for the register.
- **Names the protagonist: 57%, and 62% of shorts.** The no-names rule from every screenwriting blog is a spec-market rule; the festival-programme register names people. Luke's "an enslaved man" is the minority choice.
- **Colon or dash: 0 of 13 shorts.** Present tense: 30 of 30.

### The models named for KOM

**Sweetheart** (Sundance 2025) is KOM's skeleton already built — dated place, named man *forced* into a hidden world, then a *ceremony* he stumbles into; 36 words, two sentences. **OURIKA!** (Sundance 2023) puts the entire period-and-bondage premise in a comma-bracketed appositive so the main clause is free. **Raging Bull** has the cleanest cost clause in the corpus (one trait, two consequences, pivoting on "But"). Also: Grace, Bay of Herons, Unborn Biru; runners-up Nightjohn, Free State of Jones, Suicide by Sunlight.

### Methodological note worth keeping

Most circulating "famous loglines" are bloggers' reconstructions, not studio wordings — "Jaws in space" was the real pitch, "In space no one can hear you scream" is a tagline, Snyder's *Die Hard* line is Snyder's. All tiered in the document. Rule conflicts recorded: ScreenCraft 35–45 words/one sentence vs Final Draft 25–50 vs **Sundance's own form 75 max, one to two sentences** vs Festagent ~25 for catalogues. Nicholl disqualifies entries with no logline.
