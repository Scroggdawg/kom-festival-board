### The requirement

IFFR's page states one requirement verbatim: *"Preview copies of submitted works must be both streamable and downloadable until 7 February 2027."*

**Downloadable** is the test, and it changes which item matters. D.3.1 exists to establish exactly that fact — *"or a screenshot of the visibility and download settings"* — and D.3.3's own spec already lists **Downloadable** as one of its tiers. So:

- If YouTube's download setting is **on**, the existing link may satisfy IFFR and D.3.3 stops being urgent for this door.
- If it is **off**, D.3.3 is the only path and its Sep 15 date is real.

**D.3.1 is a minutes-long check that could delete a multi-day task.** It is now dated 2026-09-15 and retitled to carry the reason: *"…IFFR requires the preview to be streamable and downloadable, so this decides whether D.3.3 is needed at all."* That records the dependency in visible text, which is the mechanism agreed instead of a dependency field.

**I did not date it earlier than D.3.3, and that was deliberate.** The argument for sequencing it first is sound, but Sep 15 is the only date with evidence behind it; Sep 10, 11 and 12 are equally defensible and none of them come from anything. An invented date is worse than a true one plus the ordering written in words.

### A second obligation, not just a deadline

"Until 7 February 2027" is a five-month liveness commitment on whatever link goes on the submission. D.5.5 was a record of which upload went to which festival; it now also has to record **how long each link must stay live and downloadable**, with IFFR's date named. That would not have surfaced from the deadline alone.
