### Conceded: "opponent" beats "man", and my Gen 21 note was wrong

Luke: *"opponent buys clarity… It's possible to imagine that he just killed a man."* He is right. A logline is read once and fast; by sentence 3 the reader must re-attach the killing to the fight in sentence 1, and "opponent" does that in one word. The kinship payoff I was defending is already carried by *one of his own* and *their shared ancestors*.

### "means killing" — the echo objection is wrong

*Killing* in sentence 2 and *kill again* at the end is not a defect. **"Again" needs an antecedent**, and *killing* makes it lexical rather than semantic — the closing line now points at a word on the page. Three words saved and the logic tightened. Death-words fall at 14, 23 and 63: spread, not clustered.

### One open note: "his last opponent"

**"Last" means both "most recent" and "final ever."** This sentence supports both, and in a logline that closes on *whether he will kill again* the second reading is actively invited — it misleads, or it gives away an ending. **Fix for one word: "the opponent he killed" (65).** Dropping *just* costs nothing; burial rites already imply immediacy.
