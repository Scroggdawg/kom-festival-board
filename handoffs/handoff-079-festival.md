# handoff-079-festival

## Where we left off

**This turn (Sep 8, 2026, Opus 5): read the Sundance 2027 Rules PDF. The contradiction was mine, not Sundance's.**

### There was never a contradiction

*"Only projects retaining world premiere status are eligible for open submission to this category"* appears **four times in the PDF, and every instance is attached to a FEATURE category** — U.S. Narrative Feature, U.S. Documentary Feature, and the two International Feature categories (which require international premiere). Section III says it outright:

> "Open submissions to our U.S. Feature Film categories must be world premieres, while open submissions to our International Feature Films categories must be international premieres."

**Shorts are not named.** Shorts carry a looser rule: public exhibition *"prior to January 1, 2026"* excludes, then *"Projects completed after December 31, 2025 are still eligible to submit to the Festival, regardless of premiere status."* Numbered rule 11 goes further, exempting shorts *"screened at any number of festivals anywhere in the world."* (Rule 11 says Dec 31 **2026** where the category text says **2025** — an inconsistency in Sundance's own document; both readings favour this film.)

**I pulled a feature rule off the submit page and applied it to shorts.** For *Killer of Men*: a spent world premiere does **not** exclude it from Sundance shorts. Moot for 2027 (closed Aug 31), live for 2028.

### The generalisation is withdrawn

Last turn this lane said that if the exemption were not real, **every** world-premiere-only festival would come off the board, and called it a shape-of-the-campaign question. **That was speculation stacked on the misreading.** There is no new workstream, D.5.9 needs nothing, and the premiere ledger is unaffected — the world premiere is still spent, it simply matters less than claimed.

What survives is narrower and worth keeping: **"world premiere required" is frequently a FEATURE-category rule.** No festival should be written off on premiere grounds without checking whether its rule is category-specific. A checking habit, not a task.

### The process note is worth more than the finding

**WebFetch's summariser reported this PDF was about the 2009 festival and contained no rules on premiere status or completion dates. Both claims were false.** It is the 2027 document and contains every clause above. Caught only because "2009" looked wrong, which prompted extracting the text directly with PyMuPDF.

**A summariser saying "the document does not say" is not evidence that the document does not say it.** Same shape as the prose-verification lesson: a confident sentence standing in for a check. Third instance today of the same failure class.
